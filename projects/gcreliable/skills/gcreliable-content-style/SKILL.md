# G.C. Reliable Service — Content Style

The voice of the current-generation pages (model: the AC Repair page). It is
**advisory, not promotional**. The reader is a Westchester homeowner mid-decision,
and the page's job is to help them choose correctly — including choosing *not* to buy.

## The five rules

1. **Decision-support over sales.** Repeatedly and explicitly say when the reader may
   *not* need the expensive option: *"These signs do not automatically mean you need
   a new AC."* *"If another repair still makes financial sense, replacement may not be
   the first recommendation."* This honesty is the brand.
2. **Hedge honestly.** Use *may*, *can*, *depends on*, *not guaranteed*. Never promise
   savings, timelines, or outcomes. *"A newer, properly sized system may operate more
   efficiently … but lower bills are not guaranteed."*
3. **Local specificity, not local filler.** Name New Rochelle and Westchester County,
   and reach for details a local recognises: older Westchester homes, finished
   attics, additions, converted garages, upper floors on aging ductwork, humid
   consecutive-hot-day stretches. Nearby towns worth naming: Larchmont, Mamaroneck,
   Scarsdale, Pelham, Eastchester, White Plains, Harrison, Bronxville, Rye Brook.
4. **Plain language, no hype.** No "unbeatable", "luxury", "state-of-the-art",
   "cutting-edge", exclamation marks, or invented statistics. Short paragraphs (1–3
   sentences). Second person. Active voice.
5. **Earn the proof points.** Only these are established facts — use them, don't
   inflate them: serving since **1980** (**40+ years**), **24/7 emergency HVAC
   service**, **all makes and models**, New Rochelle / Westchester County.
   One more is **kit-attested but needs a client OK before reuse**: the Cooling
   Services template (`227376`) claims **EPA-certified technicians**, both in its
   trust list and as an "EPA" stat badge. It is the agency's own copy, so it is
   usable — but confirm it is current before putting it on a new page.
   Do **not** claim other certifications, brand dealerships, awards, review counts, or
   pricing that the brief doesn't supply. (Legacy pages mention Lennox/Daikin — treat
   that as unverified unless the brief repeats it.)

## Headline patterns that fit

- Problem-first: *"When the Cooling Stops, We Move Fast"*, *"Flexible Comfort Where
  Traditional HVAC Falls Short"*
- Reframing the decision: *"The Right AC Starts With More Than Square Footage"*,
  *"One Zone or Several? Choosing the Right Ductless Setup"*
- Reader-voiced FAQ headers: *"What Westchester Homeowners Ask Before Replacing an
  AC"*, *"What Homeowners Want to Know"*
- Closing: *"Start With the Right System, Not Just a New One"*, *"Put Comfort Where
  You Actually Need It"*

Sentence case with initial caps on major words. No trailing punctuation.

## Section copy shapes

- **Hero:** H1 = service + city (*"Professional AC Installation in New Rochelle,
  NY"*); a one-line sub-promise; a 2–3 sentence lead paragraph; one CTA.
- **Symptom / fit lists:** short parallel fragments, no terminal periods, 5–8 items,
  always followed by a paragraph that de-escalates (*"These signs do not
  automatically mean…"*).
- **Process steps:** a first-person-plural verb phrase as the title
  (*"We Evaluate What the New AC Will Be Working With"*) + 2–3 sentences.
- **Why-trust list:** `<strong>Claim:</strong> one supporting sentence.` — three items,
  no more.
- **FAQ:** the question exactly as a homeowner would type it; the answer opens with
  the honest short answer (*"Not necessarily."*, *"Sometimes, but not reliably in
  every layout."*), then the conditions that change it.
- **Closing CTA:** restate the payoff, then one imperative sentence naming the
  action and the company.

## Mechanics

- **Company name:** `G.C. Reliable Service` on first use in a section, `G.C. Reliable`
  thereafter. Never "GC Reliable" without the periods in body copy.
- **Phone:** `(914) 326-0726` → `tel:+19143260726`.
- **CTA labels:** action + object, uppercase is applied by the button style, so write
  them in title case — *Schedule an AC Installation Consultation*, *Schedule Your
  Appointment*, *Schedule an Appointment*.
- **Entities:** "air conditioner"/"AC" (not "A/C"), "ductless mini-split"
  (hyphenated), "ductwork", "heat pump", "zones".
- **Punctuation:** em dash `&mdash;` (spaced), `&rsquo;` for apostrophes,
  `&amp;` for ampersands inside HTML settings. Keep JSON UTF-8.
- **Internal links:** 3–6 per page, root-relative, descriptive anchors —
  `/systems/air-conditioning`, `/systems/air-conditioning/ac-repair`,
  `/systems/air-conditioning/ac-installation`, `/systems/ductless-minisplits`,
  `/systems/maintenance`, `/systems/heat-pumps`, `/schedule-appointment`,
  `/service-area/<city>`. Never `/amana/...` or `/services/...` (legacy, dead).

## Anti-patterns (seen on the legacy pages — do not revive)

*"Any HVAC company can sell you a new air conditioner. If you're lucky, they might
even hook it up correctly."* — snide competitor jabs.
*"Experience the Luxury of…"*, *"Discover the Versatility of…"* — brochure voice.
*"We'll have it fixed before you know it."* — unearned promises.
