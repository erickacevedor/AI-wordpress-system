The Right AC Starts With More Than Square Footage
Buying a new air conditioner is a major investment. Simply replacing your old system with the same size is not always the best approach.
A cooling system that made sense years ago may no longer fit the home after an attic was finished, an addition was built, windows were replaced, or insulation was upgraded. Multi-level homes can also have very different cooling demands from one floor to another.
That is why G.C. Reliable looks beyond square footage when recommending new equipment. The goal is not to install the biggest system available. It is to choose one that can cool the home properly without paying for capacity you do not need.
Signs It May Be Time for a New Air Conditioner
It may be worth comparing repair and replacement if:
Repairs are becoming more frequent or expensive
The system struggles to keep the home comfortable
Certain rooms consistently stay warmer
Cooling costs continue to climb
The AC runs for long periods without keeping up
A major component has failed
The current system no longer fits changes made to the home
These signs do not automatically mean you need a new AC. If another repair still makes financial sense, replacement may not be the first recommendation. When reliability, comfort, repair costs, or equipment condition point toward replacement, G.C. Reliable can help you compare the next step.

What Goes Into Choosing the Right AC System?
A successful installation starts before the old equipment comes out. The new AC needs to work with the home around it, not just fit into the same space as the old one.
Proper System Sizing
Bigger does not automatically mean better.
An undersized system may run constantly and still struggle during hot weather. An oversized system can cycle too quickly and may not provide the comfort or humidity control you expected.
Replacing a 3-ton system with another 3-ton system simply because that is what was there before can also repeat an old sizing mistake. Changes to insulation, windows, additions, or the home's layout can alter cooling needs over time.
That is why square footage is only one part of the sizing decision.
Existing Ductwork and Airflow
A brand-new air conditioner cannot correct every problem elsewhere in the HVAC system.
If certain rooms have always received weak airflow, that issue should be evaluated before new equipment goes in. Otherwise, you can invest in a new AC and still end up with the same uncomfortable bedroom or second floor.
Existing ductwork should be considered for condition, sizing, restrictions, and its ability to deliver enough air where the home needs it.

What Homeowners Want to Know Before Replacing an AC
How do I know what size AC my home needs?
The right size depends on more than square footage. Layout, insulation, windows, sun exposure, ceiling height, air leakage, ductwork, and other characteristics can affect the cooling load.
A professional evaluation helps determine how much capacity the home actually needs instead of automatically matching the size of the old system.
Should I repair my old AC or replace it?
Repair may make sense when the problem is relatively minor and the system has otherwise been reliable.
Replacement is worth considering when repairs are becoming frequent, the equipment is aging, comfort has declined, or an expensive component has failed.
The better choice depends on the condition of the individual system, not age alone.
How long does AC installation take?
It depends on the scope of the project.
A straightforward replacement may be less involved than adding central air where none existed or correcting ductwork, electrical, drainage, or other conditions at the same time.
Your installer should explain the expected scope before work begins.
Will a new air conditioner lower my energy bills?
A newer, properly sized system may operate more efficiently than aging equipment, but lower bills are not guaranteed.
Actual energy use also depends on weather, thermostat settings, insulation, duct performance, and how often the system runs.
The goal is to find the right balance of purchase price, efficiency, comfort, and long-term operating cost.
Do I need to replace my ductwork when I replace my AC?
Not necessarily.
Existing ductwork may continue to work well with the new system if it is appropriately sized, in good condition, and able to deliver the required airflow.
If there are leaks, restrictions, damaged sections, or long-standing comfort problems, those issues may need to be addressed as part of the installation plan.
